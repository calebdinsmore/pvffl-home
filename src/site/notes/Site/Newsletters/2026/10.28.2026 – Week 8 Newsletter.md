[[Site/Teams/@Breesley\|@Breesley]] sent Aaron Jones to [[Site/Teams/@AlexW\|@AlexW]] for Luther Burden and J.K. Dobbins. It went through Sunday afternoon, after the early games had locked. That meant that Jones's 3.1 counted for Breesley and Burden's 21.7 from Thursday night counted for Alex, while Dobbins went to Breesley in time for his 4:05 kickoff. Dobbins scored 8.3 on Breesley's bench.

**The Prodigal Dobbins returns!** Breesley dropped him in Week 3 to add Rashod Bateman. Alex grabbed him off free agency two days later, and Dobbins went on to score a touchdown in three straight games. Breesley gave up a starter to get him back.

##### [[Site/Teams/@Breesley\|@Breesley]] wins this trade.
Burden is the prize. He's caught all 12 of his targets for 225 yards in the two games since Caleb Williams came back, and FantasyPros' rest-of-season consensus ranks him WR20. Jones is RB33 and Dobbins RB41. Burden slots in right behind Jaxon Smith-Njigba as Breesley's WR2. On Sleeper's projections the two players coming in are worth about 112 points the rest of the way against Jones's 61, and the sim moves Breesley's playoff odds from 63% without the trade to 74% with it. That nearly cancels out Sunday's loss: Breesley sits just 2 points lower compared to last week.

At 2-5 and 3.8 projected wins, Alex was already down to about 1% playoff odds without the trade, so the sim barely registers it. Alex has wide receivers to spare (Justin Jefferson, Tee Higgins, A.J. Brown, Brian Thomas) and only Ashton Jeanty as a reliable running back, so going after an RB does make sense. Jones is the wrong one to bet on, though. He left Sunday's win over the Colts with a knee injury (day-to-day) that he sustained minutes (seconds?) after the trade went through, and Jordan Mason, just back from IR, already had the bigger role. Mason is now the RB1 on the Vikings depth chart. On the plus side, Jones's bye is behind him, and FantasyPros rates Minnesota's RB schedule for Weeks 8-14 the 5th easiest.

**Verdict:** a clear win for Breesley, who added a rising WR2 for a part-time RB and got a touchdown-scorer back as a bonus. For Alex, it's an okay swap on positional need that cost him the best player in the deal.
